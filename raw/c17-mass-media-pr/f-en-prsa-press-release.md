# How to Write a Press Release That Actually Gets Published

Press releases remain a key tool for gaining media coverage and building brand credibility, but only if they’re written effectively. Here’s how to ensure your press release stands out:

*   **Start with a strong headline**: Keep it under 10 words, use active verbs, and highlight news value. Avoid vague or overused buzzwords.
*   **Follow the inverted pyramid structure**: Place the most important information - Who, What, When, Where, and Why - at the top, followed by supporting details and quotes.
*   **Keep it concise**: Aim for 300-500 words with short paragraphs for readability.
*   **Tailor your pitch**: Research journalists who cover your topic and personalize your outreach to their audience.
*   **Use proper formatting**: Stick to [AP style](https://www.apstylebook.com/), include a dateline, and end with a boilerplate and contact details.
*   **Distribute strategically**: Send press releases during optimal times (Tuesday to Thursday mornings) and include multimedia elements like images or videos to boost engagement.

## Writing Headlines That Get Noticed

Your headline is the first impression - it determines whether readers dive into your content or scroll past. In fact, 8 out of 10 people don’t make it past the headline. To grab attention, your headline needs to be clear, concise, and answer the all-important question: _Why should I care?_

### Keep Headlines Short and Clear

Stick to headlines with fewer than 10 words. Why? Most newsroom systems cut headlines off after 55 characters, and Google truncates them at around 70 characters. A long headline risks losing its message entirely.

Use **active verbs** like "unveils", "reveals", or "launches" to create urgency and highlight action. For example, "Local Startup Launches App That Automates Payroll for Small Businesses" is specific and engaging. Compare that to "Company Wins Award", which is vague and uninspiring.

Avoid cluttering your headline with your company name unless it’s well-known. Instead, focus on what makes the story compelling. Skip overused buzzwords like "revolutionary" or "disruptive" - they add fluff without substance. Remember, clarity always wins over cleverness.

Now, let’s talk about making your headline _newsworthy_ enough to grab a journalist’s attention.

### Include News Value in Your Headline

Journalists look for stories that check one or more boxes: timeliness, impact, human interest, novelty, or conflict. Your headline should clearly signal which of these applies. Adding specific numbers can make a big difference - headlines with data perform up to 36% better than those without. For instance, "$10 Million Raised to Expand Clean Water Access in Flint" combines data, location, and impact in a way that grabs attention.

Put the most critical information front and center. Place your hook and keywords within the first 60 characters. That way, even if the headline gets cut off in an email preview or search result, your main point still shines through. Take this example: a Dallas nonprofit initially used "HopeHealth Announces New Dallas Clinic" and got minimal traction. After reworking it to "Free Health Clinic Opens in Dallas, Offers Care for Uninsured", the release was picked up by multiple statewide outlets and a local TV station. The key difference? The revised headline emphasized the _impact_ rather than the organization.

## How to Structure Your Press Release

Once you've crafted a compelling headline, the next step is organizing your press release in a way that grabs attention and communicates your message effectively. A clear structure ensures editors and journalists can quickly understand your news. Using the **inverted pyramid** format is key here - it places the most critical information at the top, followed by supporting details.

### Use the Inverted Pyramid Format

The inverted pyramid approach prioritizes essential facts upfront, with additional context and details following. This method ensures that your main message remains intact, even if the press release gets trimmed. Since editors often cut from the bottom up to fit space constraints, putting the key points first guarantees your core news will survive the edit.

Consider this: while everyone sees your headline, only about 70% of readers make it to the first paragraph, 50% to the second, and just 10% to the fourth. That means your opening lines must pack a punch.

> Charlie Terenzio of Newswire explains: "The inverted pyramid is a tried and true method that helps writers address the most important information at the beginning of the press release".

### Write a Strong Opening Paragraph

Your first paragraph should immediately address the **5 Ws** - Who, What, When, Where, and Why - within 50 to 75 words. Start with a dateline in AP Style, such as: "NEW YORK, Feb. 12, 2026 - ". Avoid unnecessary backstory or company history - focus on the news itself.

For example, when [Notion](https://www.notion.com/) announced a $275 million funding round in 2021, their press release highlighted the amount raised, their $10 billion valuation, the lead investor ([Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/)), and user growth metrics - all in the first paragraph. This concise, fact-driven approach earned immediate coverage from outlets like [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/), [The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/), [Forbes](https://www.forbesmagazine.com/), and [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/). A journalist could write an entire article based on that opening alone.

Stick to clear, factual language to ensure your message is easy to digest.

### Add Supporting Information in the Body

After establishing the main news, use the body of your press release to provide supporting details. Explain how the announcement connects to broader industry trends and include quotes from executives to add depth and perspective. For instance, when Zoom faced security issues in 2020, their press release led with their "90-Day Plan" for addressing concerns. This shifted the narrative from "insecure" to "proactively solving problems".

Keep paragraphs short - 2 to 3 sentences max - for better readability, especially on mobile devices. Include data, statistics, or industry benchmarks to enhance credibility. Aim for a total word count of 300 to 500 words to hold your audience's attention.

| **Pyramid Section** | **Content Focus** | **Importance** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Lead** | The 5 Ws (Who, What, When, Where, Why) | Essential |
| **The Body** | Supporting facts, quotes, and data | Important |
| **The Tail** | Boilerplate and contact details | Supplemental |

### Include Your Boilerplate and Contact Details

Wrap up your press release with a concise boilerplate - 2 to 3 sentences about your company and its relevance. This section provides background information for journalists unfamiliar with your organization.

Follow the boilerplate with clear **media contact details**, including a name, phone number, and email address. Make it easy for journalists to reach out with questions or requests for additional information. A great example is [Slack](https://slack.com/)'s 2019 direct listing announcement. Their release began with the news of their filing and decision to pursue a direct listing instead of a traditional IPO. The second paragraph explained the mechanics, making it simple for journalists to understand and report on the story.

## Matching Your Message to Your Audience

Sending a press release that doesn’t align with a journalist’s focus is a surefire way to get it rejected. In fact, **87% of magazine journalists will dismiss a pitch if it’s irrelevant to their audience or beat**. The takeaway? Do your homework and tailor your message before hitting send.

### Research the Media Outlets You're Targeting

Start by figuring out exactly who you want to reach. Look for journalists who cover topics similar to your news by checking out their recent articles. A good place to start is the masthead, where you’ll find names, titles, and beats. Social media is another goldmine for understanding a journalist’s recent work and interests.

> "A media list is the backbone of any successful PR outreach strategy. It's more than just a spreadsheet... It's your strategic roadmap to connect with the right journalists." - Media List Market

Timing is everything. Monthly magazines often plan their content as far as six months ahead, while online outlets can publish stories within hours. And since **97% of website journalists prefer email communication**, prioritize email as your outreach method. Organize your media list by outlet type, journalist beat, and audience size to make your pitches more personal and effective.

Once you’ve pinpointed the right journalists, it’s time to fine-tune your tone.

### Adjust Your Tone and Writing Style

With your media list in hand, focus on crafting a message that speaks to each outlet’s audience. For business publications, highlight industry advancements and market trends. Local newspapers? Emphasize community ties and regional importance. General interest outlets are drawn to human stories - how your news impacts everyday people.

> "Journalists ask, 'Why should my readers care?' Answer that question in your first paragraph." - Timothy Spell, VP of Strategic Sales, OpenWater

If you’re pitching trade magazines, technical language and industry-specific jargon are appropriate. But for consumer media, stick to clear, simple language. Technology journalists will want to hear about technical specs, while business journalists care about financial results and market changes. Crafting tailored versions of your news for different outlets can significantly boost your chances of coverage.

Here’s a quick breakdown of what different outlets typically look for:

| **Outlet Type** | **Primary Content Need** | **Ideal Pitch Style/Length** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Website/Digital** | Timely, SEO-friendly hooks | Email-first; focus on driving traffic |
| **Magazine** | High-res visuals; seasonal themes | Long lead times (3-6 months); focus on "the look" |
| **Newspaper** | News announcements; local data | Fast response; focus on local community impact |
| **Newsletter** | Niche data; bite-sized insights | Short (100-200 words); very targeted |

Tailoring your message to fit both the journalist and their audience is the key to standing out in a crowded inbox.

## Formatting and Distribution Guidelines

Even the most compelling press release can fall short without proper formatting and a clear distribution strategy. Once you've crafted a strong release, following standard formatting rules and targeting the right audience ensures it grabs attention. Good formatting shows journalists you understand their workflow, while smart distribution gets your news in front of the right people.

### Use Standard Formatting Rules

Most media outlets expect press releases to follow Associated Press (AP) style. As [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/) puts it:

> "Following the same press release format as AP style makes your release more appealing and easier to work with from a journalist's perspective."

Start your press release with **"FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE"** or, if necessary, **"EMBARGOED UNTIL [Date/Time]"**. Your headline should be in title case and stay under 100 characters - this length tends to attract the most views. The dateline should list the city in ALL CAPS, followed by the state abbreviation (AP style, not postal codes), and the release date. Separate the dateline from the lead paragraph using an em dash.

Write in short paragraphs of two to three sentences. Use single spacing after periods, and skip the Oxford comma in simple lists. For numbers, spell out one through nine and use numerals for 10 and above, unless you're referencing ages, percentages, or dimensions. When abbreviating months, only shorten Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., and Dec. if they appear with a specific date.

End your press release with a boilerplate - a brief summary about your organization - under 100 words, followed by media contact details. Use **"###"** or **"Ends"** to indicate the conclusion. If you have additional materials, like high-resolution images or interview opportunities, include a "Notes to Editors" section after the contact information.

Once your press release is formatted, it’s time to focus on distribution.

### Use Distribution Tools to Reach Journalists

A layered approach works best for press release distribution. Combine direct, personalized pitches for niche stories with newswire services for broader reach. Use media databases to find journalists who cover specific topics, outlets, or regions, ensuring your press release lands in the right inboxes.

Timing matters, too. Research shows that 44% of journalists prefer receiving pitches between 5 a.m. and noon in their local time zone. The sweet spot? Tuesday through Thursday mornings, ideally at an off-peak time like 11:17 a.m., to help your pitch stand out. If you plan to follow up, wait four to six days - 85% of journalists are fine with follow-ups after about a week.

Kristen Dunleavy, Senior Content Marketing Manager at [Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/), emphasizes this point:

> "Effective distribution is how you turn your announcement into coverage."

Multimedia can also make a big difference. Including one photo can double engagement compared to text-only press releases, and adding multiple images can increase engagement by up to six times. Videos are another powerful tool, driving about three times more engagement than text alone. To fine-tune your strategy, track performance metrics like open rates, link clicks, and media pickups using analytics tools.

## Conclusion

### Summary of Key Strategies

Creating a press release that gets published comes down to nailing some key elements. Start with a **headline under 100 characters** that grabs attention, uses active voice, and skips the marketing buzzwords . In your opening paragraph, make sure to cover the five Ws - _who, what, when, where, and why_ - in just 50 to 75 words, giving journalists the essential details they need to build a story. Aim to keep the entire release between **300 and 500 words**, with short paragraphs (three to four sentences) to make it easy to skim .

The structure of your press release is just as critical as the content. Use the **inverted pyramid format**, starting with the most important information, followed by supporting details, and wrapping up with your boilerplate and contact info . Stick to **AP style formatting** to show you understand how journalists work . Add quotes that bring depth or emotion to the story instead of repeating facts, and include multimedia elements like **high-quality images or videos**, which can boost engagement by up to six times compared to text alone.

Timing and distribution are equally important. Send your press release on **Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday mornings**, when journalists are more likely to engage, and tailor your outreach to **10 to 20 relevant reporters** who cover your topic . If you don’t hear back, follow up after a week - 85% of journalists are okay with this timeframe.

Mastering these strategies will give you a solid foundation for crafting press releases that stand out and get noticed.

## FAQs

### What counts as “newsworthy” enough for a press release?

A press release becomes _newsworthy_ when it connects with your audience by being relevant, timely, impactful, or offering something fresh and engaging. Think about covering major announcements, breakthroughs, or events that tie into current trends or issues people care about. Stories with a human angle or emotional connection can also grab attention. Always address the key question: **"Why does this matter now, and who should care?"** Answering that ensures your press release resonates and stands out.

### How do I find the right journalists to pitch my release to?

Finding the right journalists starts with focusing on those who cover your industry or topic. Dive into their recent articles to understand their interests and the specific areas they write about. This helps ensure your pitch aligns with their beat.

Media databases can be a goldmine for identifying journalists who’ve written about similar stories. These tools often allow you to search by topic, outlet, or even specific keywords, making it easier to pinpoint the right contacts.

When reaching out, _personalization is key._ Reference their previous work to show you’ve done your homework and explain why your story resonates with their audience. A thoughtful, tailored approach can make your pitch stand out.

Lastly, think about the long game. Building genuine relationships - whether through social media interactions or direct engagement - can increase the likelihood of your story being picked up, not just once, but in the future too.

### Should I use a newswire or email reporters directly?

When deciding how to share your news, it all comes down to your goals. **Newswires** are a great option if you need to distribute time-sensitive or impactful announcements quickly and to a wide audience. They can boost visibility, but keep in mind that generic releases might not grab the attention of journalists.

On the other hand, **emailing reporters directly** gives you the chance to personalize your outreach. By tailoring your message to match a reporter's specific interests, you can increase the chances of engagement and meaningful coverage.

For the best results, consider combining both approaches. Use newswires for broad distribution while also sending targeted emails to individual journalists. This way, you can maximize exposure and start building stronger relationships with the media.
