_**Pantun**_ ([Arab Melayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Melayu_script?action=edit&redlink=1 "Arab Melayu script (page does not exist)"): ڤنتون) is a [Malayic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayic "Malayic") oral [poetic form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetic_form "Poetic form") used to express intricate ideas and emotions.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-1) It generally consists of an even number of lines[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-2) and is based on {\displaystyle \mathrm {ABAB} }![Image 4: {\displaystyle \mathrm {ABAB} }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/c6babf56bde8423944ae9d9e3899493b9b3a53e3)[rhyming schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyming_scheme "Rhyming scheme").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-3) The shortest pantun consists of two lines, known as the _pantun dua kerat_ in Malay, while the longest, the _pantun enam belas kerat_, can have 16 lines.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-4)

Pantun is a disjunctive form of poetry that always comes in two parts: the first part is a prefatory statement called _pembayang_ or _sampiran_ which has no immediate logical or narrative connection with the second or closing statement called _maksud_ or _isi_.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-6)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-8)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-9) However, they are always connected by rhyme and other verbal associations, such as [puns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pun "Pun") and [repeating sounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliteration "Alliteration").[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-10) There is also an oblique but necessary relationship, and the first statement often serves as a metaphor for the second.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-11) The most popular forms of pantun are the quatrain (four lines)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-12) and the couplet (two lines),[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-13) which both feature prominently in literature and modern popular culture.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-14)

The earliest literary records of pantun date back to the 15th century [Malacca Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate "Malacca Sultanate"),[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-15) although some historians believe that pantun may be as old as, or even precede, Classical Malay itself, having grown and spread during the [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya "Srivijaya") era, from which the founder of Malacca originated. Pantun during the Malacca era was featured in the most important Malay literary text, the [Malay Annals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Annals "Malay Annals"),[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-16) and is regarded as a high art integral to [classical Malay literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Malay_literature "Classical Malay literature"). It also thrived naturally in the daily communication of traditional [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_(ethnic_group) "Malays (ethnic group)") society and served as an important expressive tool in Malay songs,[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-17) rituals, performing arts, and all forms of storytelling.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-18)

## Etymology

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pantun&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Etymology")]

According to [Za'aba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zainal_Abidin_Ahmad_(writer) "Zainal Abidin Ahmad (writer)"), the word _pantun_ is thought to have evolved from the Malay word _sepantun_ ([Jawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawi_alphabet "Jawi alphabet"): سڤنتون), meaning 'same as'.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-19) This word is used to signify a proverbial metaphor or [simile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simile "Simile"),[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-20) a type of [figure of speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_of_speech "Figure of speech") commonly found in traditional pantun or [proverbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proverb "Proverb") from [classical Malay literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Malay_literature "Classical Malay literature").[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-21) The archaic meaning of *pantun* in Malay also refers to a form of [proverb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proverb "Proverb") used for indirect references,[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-22) which has a similar role to pantun as poetry, still generally created in styles portraying _sindir_ (indirect references) and _kias_ (analogies).[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-23)

Another theory suggests that pantun originated from the word _penuntun_ ('guider'),[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-24) from the noun-building prefix _pe(n)_ and the verb _tuntun_ ([Jawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawi_alphabet "Jawi alphabet"): تونتون) or 'to guide'.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-25) Alternatively, [Brandstetter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renward_Brandstetter "Renward Brandstetter") suggested that the word originates from _tun_ and its similar sounding variants in [Austronesian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages "Austronesian languages"), with multiple meanings: [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language "Kapampangan language")_tuntun_ ('well organized'), [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language "Tagalog language")_tonton_ ('skillful arrangement'), [Old Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawi_language "Kawi language")_tuntun_ ('thread'), _atuntun_ ('well arranged'), _matuntun_ ('to lead'), and [Toba Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toba_Batak_language "Toba Batak language")_pantun_ ('polite' or 'worthy of respect'). [Winstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Olaf_Winstedt "Richard Olaf Winstedt") supported this opinion, noting that in many Austronesian languages, words suggesting 'something set out in rows' gradually acquired the new meaning of 'well-arranged words', in prose or in poetry.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-26) Ari Welianto suggested that pantun originated from the [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_language "Minangkabau language") word _patuntun_, which means "guide".[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-Pantun-27)

Linguist Tom Hoogervorst proposes that _pantun_ may have been derived from a fossilized form of [Old Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Javanese "Old Javanese")_parik_ meaning "together" suffixed with _-ntən_ indicating a [formal (_krama_)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language#Registers "Javanese language")[register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Register_(sociolinguistics) "Register (sociolinguistics)") corresponding to Malay _-ntan_ or the [Banjarese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjarese_language "Banjarese language")_-ntang_.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-28)

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pantun&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: History")]

Some scholars believe that _pantuns_ predates literacy and may be as old as the Malay language itself.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-29) Muhammad Haji Salleh believes that the _pantuns_ form grew and spread from [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya "Srivijaya"), most probably from around the cities of [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang "Palembang") or the [Malayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melayu_Kingdom "Melayu Kingdom"). As [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang "Palembang") became more dominant, the _pantuns_ of the two cities would have been known to each other's populations, despite their political rivalry, as they used the same language.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-30) Nevertheless, the tradition is known to have reached its refined form with the flowering of classical Malay literature from the 15th century.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-31)[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-32) Notable literary works like the [Malay Annals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Annals "Malay Annals") and [Hikayat Hang Tuah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hikayat_Hang_Tuah "Hikayat Hang Tuah") contain the earliest written examples of _pantun_.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-33)[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-34)

For at least 500 years, pantun spread through [Malay language tranmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayisation "Malayisation") via trade routes, ports, and migrations, becoming the most dynamic single literary form.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-35) Today, it is known in at least 40 dialects of Malay and 35 non-Malay languages in the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula "Malay Peninsula") and many islands of [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia "Maritime Southeast Asia").[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-36) The popularity of pantun among hybrid communities like the [Peranakans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peranakans "Peranakans"), [Chitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitty "Chitty"), and [Kristang people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristang_people "Kristang people"), signifies its prominent position as a cultural symbol in the [Malay world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_world "Malay world").[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-37) A type of pantun called _pantun berkait_, consisting of interwoven quatrains, was introduced to Western poetry in the 19th century by [Ernest Fouinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Fouinet "Ernest Fouinet") and later popularised by [Victor Hugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Hugo "Victor Hugo"), forming the basis of the modern [pantoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantoum "Pantoum").[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-38)

## Tradition

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pantun&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Tradition")]

The _pantun_ originated as a traditional oral form of expression, manifesting the traditional [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_people "Malay people")'s views of life and their surroundings, and utilized to express a wide range of emotions and ideas. As a symbol of [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_people "Malay people") identity, _pantuns_ are seen as reflections of _[adat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adat "Adat")_ ('customs') and _[adab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adab\_(Islam) "Adab (Islam)")_ ('manners'). As Malay culture emphasizes polite and indirect expressions, _pantun_ are generally created using various forms of figurative language.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-39) Elements of metaphors, similes, symbols, personifications, eponyms, allusions, idioms, and proverbs are abundant in the elegantly compacted Malay _pantuns_.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-40)

In Malay culture, _pantun_ is an important instrument of communication in various social, cultural, and economic activities. It is traditionally used to express feelings, give advice, exchange quizzes, and sweeten conversations. For example, _pantuns_ are used in the customary verbal exchange in a Malay wedding (or engagement) ceremony, especially as part of the culturally sanctioned greetings between representatives of the bridegroom and bride upon arrival at the bride's house.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-41) As an expressive tool, pantuns are also used extensively in the lyrics of traditional Malay songs[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-42) tuned to popular rhythms like [Zapin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapin "Zapin"),[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-43)[Inang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mak_Inang "Mak Inang"), and [Joget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joget "Joget").[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-44) Other notable applications of _pantuns_ can be found as a structural support for art performances like [Dondang sayang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dondang_sayang "Dondang sayang"), [Bangsawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsawan "Bangsawan"), [Mak yong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mak_yong "Mak yong"), [Mek Mulung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mek_Mulung "Mek Mulung"), and [Dikir barat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikir_barat "Dikir barat").[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-45) The skill in performing these poems lies in reciting them in a way that suggests singing while simultaneously demonstrating the ability to engage in quick, witty, and subtle dialogue.

Outside [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Indonesians "Malay Indonesians")-populated areas of the Indonesian archipelago, there are the _kentrung_ traditions of central and eastern [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java") using structures similar to the _pantun_ (called _parikan_) to recount religious or local historical tales to the accompaniment of a drum.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-46) Records from linguists in the early 20th century like Hans Frederich Overbeck and Joannes Jacobus De Hollander have no mention of this artform, so that appears to be a newer adaptation coming later after their period.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-47) Indeed, much of [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia")'s traditional literature forms the foundation of complex mixed-genre performances, such as the [Randai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randai "Randai") of the [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people "Minangkabau people") of [western Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sumatra "West Sumatra"), which blends instrumental music, dance, drama, and martial arts in ceremonial settings.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-48)

## Description

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pantun&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Description")]

In its most basic form, the _pantun_ consists of a quatrain employing an ABAB [rhyme scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyme_scheme "Rhyme scheme"). A _pantun_ is traditionally recited according to a fixed rhythm; as a rule of thumb, to maintain the rhythm, every line should contain between eight and 12 syllables. According to Katharine Sim, "The _pantun_ is a four-lined verse consisting of alternating, roughly rhyming lines. The first and second lines sometimes appear completely disconnected in meaning from the third and fourth, but there is almost invariably a link of some sort. Whether it be a mere association of ideas, or of feeling, expressed through [assonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assonance "Assonance") or through the faintest nuance of thought, it is nearly always traceable" (Sim, page 12). The _pantun_ is highly allusive, and to understand it, readers generally need to know the traditional meaning of the symbols the poem employs. An example (followed by a translation by Katharine Sim):[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-FOOTNOTESim1987-49)

> _Tanam selasih di tengah padang,  
> Sudah bertangkai diurung semut,  
>  Kita kasih orang tak sayang,  
> Halai-balai tempurung hanyut._  
>   
>  I planted sweet basil in mid-field  
> Grown, it swarmed with ants,  
>  I loved but am not loved,  
> I am all confused and helpless.

According to Sim, _halai-Balai tempurung hanyut_ literally means 'a floating coconut shell at sixes and sevens'. _Selasih_ ('[sweet basil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_basil "Sweet basil")') implies 'lover' because it rhymes with the word for that, _kekasih_. Other frequently recurring symbols are the flower and the bee, indicating a girl and her lover, the squirrel (_tupai_) implying a seducer, and the [water hyacinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_hyacinth "Water hyacinth") (_bunga kiambang_) meaning love that will not take root. The _pantun_ often makes use of proverbs as well as geographical and historical allusions, for example, the following poem by [Munshi Abdullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_bin_Abdul_Kadir "Abdullah bin Abdul Kadir"):[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantun#cite_note-FOOTNOTESim198740-50)

> _Singapura negeri baharu,  
> Tuan Raffles menjadi raja,  
>  Bunga melur, cempaka biru,  
> Kembang sekuntum di mulut naga._  
>   
>  Singapore is a new country,  
> Tuan Raffles has become its lord,  
>  Chinese jasmine, purple magnolia,  
> A burgeon of flower in the dragon's mouth.

This alludes to the foundation of [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore") in 1819 by [Sir Stamford Raffles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Stamford_Raffles "Sir Stamford Raffles"): Sim suggests the "flower on the dragon's mouth" an allusion to Raffles's wife, [Olivia Mariamne Devenish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Mariamne_Devenish "Olivia Mariamne Devenish") protected under Stamford's own power.

Sometimes a _pantun_ may consist of a series of interwoven quatrains, in which case it is known as a _pantun berkait_. This follows the {\displaystyle \mathrm {ABAB} }![Image 5: {\displaystyle \mathrm {ABAB} }](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/media/math/render/svg/c6babf56bde8423944ae9d9e3899493b9b3a53e3) rhyme scheme, with the second and fourth lines of each [stanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanza "Stanza") becoming the first and third lines of the following stanza. Finally, the first and third lines of the first stanza become the second and fourth lines of the last stanza, usually in reverse order, so that the first and last lines of the poem are identical. This form of _pantun_ has exercised the most influence on Western literature, where it is known as the _[pantoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantoum "Pantoum")_.
