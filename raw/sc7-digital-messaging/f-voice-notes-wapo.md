Kelle Hampton loves the suspense when she sees three dots pop up in her phone messages. But the 46-year-old author and content creator isn’t expecting a text. She’s waiting as her friend records the next installment of a multipart voice note.

[![Image 2](https://arc-anglerfish-washpost-prod-washpost.s3.amazonaws.com/public/PGUBVUJQIRFYLF6QQYYJ6OXJAA.png)Get concise answers to your questions. Try Ask The Post AI.](https://www.washingtonpost.com/ask-the-post-ai/)

For some people with packed schedules and a penchant for human connection, voice notes have emerged as their preferred form of communication, allowing them the freedom to talk like no one’s listening (yet), and convey all the feels and hot goss that fail to translate over text. In 2023, about 62 percent of Americans said they’ve sent a voice note, and 30 percent said they communicated with voice messages daily, weekly or multiple times a day, according to a poll from [YouGov and Vox](https://www.vox.com/technology/23665101/voice-message-whatsapp-apple-text). Voice memo devotees say they love [freely unloading their thoughts](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2024/05/20/voice-memos-everywhere/) to close friends and family without fussing over how unpolished or rambling they sound.
