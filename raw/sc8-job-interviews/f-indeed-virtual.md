## Online interview tips

Preparation is a critical element for any job interview. Consider following these steps to prepare effectively for an online interview:
### 1. Test the technology

Familiarize yourself with the necessary equipment and software before your appointment. Ensure the microphone and speakers are working and your internet connection supports high-quality live video by making or receiving a test call from a friend or family member. You may also want to make sure you're using a professional screen name that interviewers can easily recognize. 
### 2. Practice

After checking your technology with a friend or family member, ask them to [practice interview questions](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/job-interview-practice) over the video call. Rehearse responding to questions naturally and letting the interviewer finish before speaking. Practice listening carefully, speaking clearly and leaving space after they finish since video calls usually have a slight delay. The more you practice, the more confident you'll be for the actual call.
### 3. Consider your location

Before the scheduled interview time, select an appropriate location. Find a quiet room with minimal background noise and no distractions. Ensure you have adequate lighting (preferably natural) and that nothing behind you is distracting. Tell anyone else in the house you'll be in an interview, and shut pets out of the room. Taking these steps to present a professional atmosphere will show the interviewer you are serious about the interview and the job.Related:[How To Use a Virtual Background in Zoom Interviews](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/zoom-interview-background)
### 4. Take notes

Keeping a notebook and pen next to your computer allows you to quickly write down important information during the interview. You can also open a document and [take notes](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/how-to-take-notes) on the computer. Whichever method you choose, alert the interviewer that you're taking notes so they don't mistake your activity for disinterest. As long as they know what you're doing, writing down ideas and details can show your interest and engagement. Taking notes also allows you to reference key points in a follow-up email or second interview and reminds you of any questions or follow-up comments you want to make.
### 5. Ready a resume

Consider printing out a copy of your resume to consult during the interview. You can also pull up a copy on your computer as long as it doesn't obscure your view of the interviewer. Highlighting important dates or job responsibilities gives you quick reference points to discuss when an interviewer asks you a question. You can also highlight key points during the interview to give you insight into what employers find most important on your resume.Related: [7 Items To Bring to a Job Interview](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/what-to-bring-to-a-job-interview)
### 6. Prepare your own questions

In addition to preparing answers to common interview questions, it's also a good idea to[prepare several questions](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/questions-to-ask-in-an-interview)of your own for a prospective employer. Ask the interviewer about job requirements or company values. You can also ask specific questions about their benefits package and salary amount. Asking questions that allow the interviewer to share additional information about what they expect from the ideal candidate can aid you in the next stage of interviewing.
### 7. Start early

Just as you would in a face-to-face interview, prepare to start your interview before the actual time. Opening your online platform a few minutes before your interview shows professional courtesy and gives you time to fix problems with your technology. Although the interviewer may wait until the scheduled time to open the video call, showing you're ready to start can make a positive first impression on a hiring manager.
### 8. Dress professionally

Although you may be participating in an online interview from home, wearing professional attire can help you focus and give you confidence. Wear clean clothes and style your hair as you would for an in-person interview to feel more confident and communicate your professionalism when meeting with an employer. Whether you're meeting with an interviewer online or in person, choose something that reflects your personality and taste while maintaining your professionalism.
### 9. Practice active listening

To strengthen your interaction with hiring personnel, use [active listening techniques](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/active-listening-skills) to show your engagement. While listening, indicate your understanding with an affirmative nod or word. Active listening also involves letting the other person finish completely and processing what you hear before responding with thoughtful answers. Ask clarifying questions or restate their question if you need time to think or formulate your answer.
### 10. Focus on the interviewer

Throughout the interview, keep your attention focused on the hiring manager by looking at the webcam instead of your video image. Check the settings during your technology test and turn off your view of yourself if that's less distracting. If you look away to take a note or glance at your resume, return your attention to the camera as soon as possible. This allows the interviewer to see your face as they would during an in-person interview.
### 11. Have backup ready

When preparing for an online interview, ensure you have backup equipment and resources available. For example, have a backup internet or hotspot, an extra device such as a laptop, tablet or cell phone, and a pair of headphones ready in case there's an issue with your main connection or device. In such instances, you can use the backup resources to continue with the interview.
### 12. Stretch or exercise first

When preparing for a video interview, you may have the advantage of being in your home or a private space before the meeting. This allows you the time and privacy to do some light exercise or stretching before the interview. Exerting some energy before the video call can help boost your confidence, calm your nerves and keep you focused.
### 13. Make sure you're prepared for the interviewer's questions

When preparing for your online interview, you may want to memorize the questions a hiring manager may ask you along with your prospective answers. You can write the potential interview questions with their respective answers and practice them before you meet with an employer. This would be an excellent use of your practice time with a friend or family, especially if you share the questions ahead of time and let them conduct a [mock interview](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/mock-job-interview).Related:[Common Interview Questions and How To Answer Them](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/top-interview-questions-and-answers)
### 14. Consider wearing earbuds

Tucking an earbud into your ear can help you hear interviewers more clearly, even if you're alone in a room at home. These devices also are smaller than regular headphones, which may be distracting for interviewers. Practice speaking with your earbuds in to balance your vocal volume.
### 15. Arrange care for children and pets

Even if you're conducting the video interview from home, planning child and/or pet care is a good idea to minimize distractions during your call. You may want to schedule at least an hour with your caregiver to allow yourself time to calm down and focus before an interview. If you ask the caregiver to come to your home, have them tend to your child or pet away from where you plan to interview.
### 16. Send a follow-up

After your online interview, email your interviewer and thank them for taking the time to meet with you. Mention what most excites you about the opportunity and include a few details about what you learned during the call. If the interviewer asks you to send additional documents or more information, prepare your response quickly after you finish the interview and include it with the [thank-you note](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/sample-thank-you-letter-after-interview).

## Frequently asked questions

Here are answers to some of the most common questions about video interviews.
### How do I handle time zone differences when scheduling a video interview?

When scheduling a video interview across different time zones, you should clearly display the time zone in every message and invitation, utilizing full location names or UTC offsets instead of short labels. It is also important to watch for daylight saving time changes and send a confirmation 24 hours prior that includes the meeting link and both parties' local times. Additionally, you can utilize automated scheduling tools or pick interview windows that focus on shared overlap periods.
### What should I do if the interviewer doesn't join the video call at the scheduled time?

If the interviewer does not join a video call at the scheduled time, first confirm the date, time, time zone and meeting link, then check your internet, audio and video connection. If everything looks correct, wait briefly and send a polite email or message asking whether the meeting is still going ahead or needs to be rescheduled.
### How should I prepare differently for a panel or group video interview?

To prepare for a panel or group video interview, gather information about the panel ahead of time and thoroughly research each panel member's professional background and role. This comprehensive research allows you to anticipate their unique perspective and tailor your responses to align with different stakeholders' interests. Additionally, prepare multiple copies of your resume for all members and plan specific questions to ask the panel to demonstrate your investment in the position.
### How can I request accessibility accommodations for a video interview?

To request accessibility accommodations for a video interview, contact the employer or hiring team as early as possible and explain the accommodation you need for the interview process. You do not need to share unrelated medical details; focus on the change that would allow you to participate fully and effectively.
### What is the best way to set up lighting and camera angle for a professional appearance?

Position your camera at eye level so that you are looking directly at your interviewer and not down at them. For lighting, use bright overhead or indirect front-facing light to illuminate your face and avoid backlighting, which can turn you into a silhouette. This setup ensures you appear engaged, professional and clearly visible throughout the video interview.
