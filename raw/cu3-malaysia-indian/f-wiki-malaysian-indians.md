**Malaysian Indians** or **Indo-Malaysians** are [Malaysian citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysians "Malaysians") of [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") ancestry. Most are descendants of those who migrated from India to [British Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya "British Malaya") from the mid-19th to the mid-20th centuries.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-4) Most Malaysian Indians are ethnic [Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Malaysians "Tamil Malaysians"); smaller groups include the [Malayalees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malayali "Malaysian Malayali"), [Telugus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Telugu "Malaysian Telugu") and [Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Malaysians "Punjabi Malaysians"). Malaysian Indians form the fifth-largest community of [Overseas Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-resident_Indian_and_person_of_Indian_origin "Non-resident Indian and person of Indian origin") in the world.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-themalaymailonline.com-5) In Malaysia, they represent the third-largest group, constituting 7% of the Malaysian population, after the [Bumiputera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumiputera_(Malaysia) "Bumiputera (Malaysia)") (combined grouping of ethnic [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malays "Malaysian Malays") and [other indigenous groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Asal "Orang Asal")) and the [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Chinese "Malaysian Chinese").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-2023_population-1) They are usually referred to simply as "Indians" in English, _Orang India_ in Malay, and _Yinduren_ in Chinese.

Malaysia's Indian population is notable for its class stratification, with a significant elite and a large low income group within its fold.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-malaymail2015-6)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-7) Malaysian Indians large percentage of professionals per capita by constituting 15.5% of Malaysia's professionals in 1999 has been reduced with substantial population close to 40% is now considered the B40 category.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Haque-8)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-malaymail2015-6) In the 1984 census, up to 38% of the nation's medical professional workforce consisted of Malaysian Indians, but this has been since been reduced.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Haque-8)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:0-9) In 1970, the per-capita income of Malaysian Indians was 76% higher than that of the Malay majority.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Malaysiaincome-10) Despite attempts by the Malaysian government to redistribute wealth since the 1970s through institutionalized racial policy,[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:1-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:4-12) by 2005 Malaysian Indians still earned a 27% higher per capita income than that of the dominant Malay community.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Malaysiaincome-10)

## History

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

### First Wave: Pre-colonial period

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: First Wave: Pre-colonial period")]

Main article: [Greater India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India "Greater India")

[![Image 8](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Candi_Batu_Pahat_of_Bujang_Valley.jpg/250px-Candi_Batu_Pahat_of_Bujang_Valley.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Candi_Batu_Pahat_of_Bujang_Valley.jpg)

Candi Bukit Batu Pahat in [Bujang Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bujang_Valley "Bujang Valley"). A Hindu-Buddhist kingdom ruled ancient [Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedah "Kedah") possibly as early as 110 A.D, this might be the evidence of Hindu-Buddhist influence before the onset of Islam in Kedah's [Kedahan Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedahan_Malay "Kedahan Malay").

[Ancient India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_History "Indian History") exerted a profound influence over Southeast Asia through trade, religious missions, wars and other forms of contact. Pre-colonial Malaysia was part of the Malay Kingdoms with Hindu-Buddhist influence such as [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya "Srivijaya"), and the [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit "Majapahit"), which formed part of a cultural region known as India. There is a possibility that the first wave of migration from India towards Southeast Asia happened during Asoka's invasion towards Kalinga and [Samudragupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudragupta "Samudragupta")'s expedition towards the South.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-13)

The Arab and Indian traders had travelled this region including the southern tip of South East Asia the peninsula with maritime trade,[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Sneddon2003-14) the [Sailendra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailendra "Sailendra") kings of [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java "Java") were able to take control of the [Peninsular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia "Peninsular Malaysia") and part of southern [Siam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siam "Siam"). The kings welcomed Buddhist missionaries from India, accepting their teaching of the [Mahayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana "Mahayana") sect, which spread through their territories. However, central and northeastern Thailand continued to adhere to the Hinayana teachings of the Theravada sect, which had been introduced by missionaries sent by the emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BC. Another theory of the introduction of Buddhism after Indian arrived in the peninsula is that after Kalinga conquered lower [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma "Burma") in the 8th century their influence gradually spread down the peninsula. The ancient Indian Kalinga was located in southeastern India occupying modern day Orissa and northern Andhra Pradesh. In the 7th century an Indonesian kingdom was named Kalingga[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-15) after the aforementioned Kalinga in India. Chinese sources mention this kingdom (_Holing_) as a center for Buddhist scholars around 604 before it was overshadowed by the Sanjaya or [Mataram kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mataram_kingdom "Mataram kingdom"). The most famous Kalingga ruler is Ratu Sima.

There is evidence of the existence of [Indianised kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianised_kingdom "Indianised kingdom") such as [Gangga Negara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangga_Negara "Gangga Negara"), Old [Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedah "Kedah"), [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya "Srivijaya") since approximately 1700 years ago.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Volume_7_2009-16) Early contact between the kingdoms of [Tamilakkam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Tamil_country "Ancient Tamil country") and the Malay peninsula had been very close during the reigns of the [Pallava dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallava_dynasty "Pallava dynasty") (from the 4th to the 9th century CE) and [Chola dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty "Chola dynasty") (from the 9th to the 13th century CE). The trade relations the Tamil merchants had with the ports of [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula "Malay Peninsula") led to the emergence of Indianised kingdoms like Kadaram ([Old Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bujang_Valley "Bujang Valley")) and [Langkasugam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkasuka "Langkasuka").[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-International2000-17) Furthermore, Chola king [Rajendra Chola I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Chola_I "Rajendra Chola I") sent an expedition to Kadaram ([Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya "Srivijaya")) during the 11th century conquering that country on behalf of one of its rulers who sought his protection and to have established him on the throne. The Cholas had a powerful [merchant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_Navy "Merchant Navy") and [naval fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_fleet "Naval fleet") in the Indian Ocean and the [Bay of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal "Bay of Bengal"). Three kinds of craft are distinguished by the author of the _Periplus_ – light coasting boats for local traffic, larger vessels of a more complicated structure and greater carrying capacity, and lastly the big ocean-going vessels that made the voyages to Malaya, [Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra "Sumatra"), and the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges "Ganges").[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Sastri2000-18) In [Malacca Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate "Malacca Sultanate"), the [Chitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitty "Chitty") people, played a huge role in Malacca's administration of the local ports such as [Raja Mudaliar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Mudaliar "Raja Mudaliar"), Syahbandar (Chief of Port) of Malacca and [Bendahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendahara "Bendahara")[Tun Mutahir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tun_Mutahir_of_Malacca "Tun Mutahir of Malacca"), a famous [Bendahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendahara "Bendahara") of the [Malaccan Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Malacca "Sultanate of Malacca").

### Second Wave: Colonial period

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Second Wave: Colonial period")]

[![Image 9](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/SS_Rajula.jpg/250px-SS_Rajula.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:SS_Rajula.jpg)

_SS Rajula_, operating her fortnightly "Straits Service" between [Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras "Madras") to Penang, [Port Klang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Klang "Port Klang") and Singapore from 1926 to 1972. The vessel transported many Indian migrants between South India to then-[British Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya "British Malaya") as well as independent Malaysia.

Following the Portuguese colonisation of [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Malacca "Sultanate of Malacca") ([Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia")) in 1511, the Portuguese government encouraged their explorers to bring their married Indian women who were converted already to Roman Catholic Christianity, under a policy set by [Afonso de Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_de_Albuquerque "Afonso de Albuquerque"), then [Viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy "Viceroy") of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India "Portuguese India"). These people were [Goan Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goan_Catholics "Goan Catholics") ([Konkani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkani_people "Konkani people") Catholics) and [Bombay East Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_East_Indians "Bombay East Indians") (Catholics of [Marathi descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_people "Marathi people")). [Kuparis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kupari "Kupari") who were of mixed Samvedic Brahmin, Goan and Portuguese descent also arrived. Their children already intermarried with Malay population, losing their ethnic identities. British acquisition of [Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang "Penang"), [Melaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melaka "Melaka"), and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore") - the Straits Settlements from 1786 to 1824 started a steady inflow of Indian labour. This consisted of traders, policemen, plantation labourers and colonial soldiers (see [sepoys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepoy "Sepoy")). Apart from this there was also substantial migration of Indians to work in the British colonial government, due to their general good command of the English language.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Volume_7_2009-16)

[![Image 10](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/The_Indian_Stick_Display_at_Balik_Pulau%2C_Penang_in_1937.jpg/250px-The_Indian_Stick_Display_at_Balik_Pulau%2C_Penang_in_1937.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Indian_Stick_Display_at_Balik_Pulau,_Penang_in_1937.jpg)

[Thaipusam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaipusam "Thaipusam") Celebration in [Balik Pulau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balik_Pulau "Balik Pulau"), [Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang "Penang"). 1937

The establishment of the plantations and the need for mass labour led to an influx of Indian migrants working under the indenture [Kangani system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangani_system "Kangani system") in the 19th and early 20th century. Some, after the Kangani system ended in the early 20th century, also paid for their own passage to Malaya. These migrant workers were primarily Tamils (around 80%), with some [Telugus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugus "Telugus"), [Malayalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayali "Malayali") as well as Bengalis from eastern India and Punjabis from western India. The Tamil migrants were primarily from Tamil Nadu.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-rajoo-19) These workers were distinct from the commercial and educated group of urban Indians, who were often Tamils, Telugus, Malayali, [Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabis "Punjabis"), and [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis "Bengalis"). The [Gujaratis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujaratis "Gujaratis"), [Sindhis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhis "Sindhis") and [Marwaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwaris "Marwaris") arrived as "passenger Indians" much like in other British colonies. These urban Indians may be Hindus, Christians, Sikhs or Muslims. The [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs "Sikhs") mainly worked in the police force, while other northern Indians were involved in business. The [Malayalees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malayali "Malaysian Malayali"), Ceylonese and Christian Tamils may be involved in government and private [white-collar work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-collar_worker "White-collar worker"), the [Chettiar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chettiar "Chettiar") in money-lending or finance, while the [Vellalar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vellalar "Vellalar") and [Muslim Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Tamils "Muslim Tamils") may be in various kind of businesses.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-20)

The Indian population in [pre-independence Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya "British Malaya") and Singapore was predominantly adult males who were single or with family back in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India "India") and [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka"). Hence the population fluctuated frequently with periods of immigration to Malaya and periods of exodus of people back to India. As early as 1901 the Indian population in the Straits Settlements and the Federated Malay States was approximately 120,000. By 1931 there were 640,000 Indians in Malaya and Singapore and they even outnumbered the native [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_(ethnic_group) "Malays (ethnic group)") in the state of [Selangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selangor "Selangor") that year.

However, during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") many Indian men and women left for [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma "Burma") as part of the [Indian National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Army "Indian National Army") with thousands thought to have perished. As a result, the population of Indians in 1957 had only increased to 820,000. While immigration was a major factor for the increase in population until Independence, the population growth began falling after that as the white collar classes in the civil service and plantations left when British institutions and companies left the country. Since then, lower birth rates and emigration to countries like [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"), [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK "UK"), etc. in search of better educational and economic opportunities meant that Indians continue to see their share of Malaysia's population decline just as is the case with the [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people "Chinese people"). Today, Malaysian Indians account for approximately 7 per cent of the total population of Malaysia (approx. 2 million) and 9 per cent in Singapore (450,000).

### Third Wave: Contemporary period

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Third Wave: Contemporary period")]

From the 1990s to the present period, there has also been a much smaller wave of Indian nationals into Singapore and Malaysia to work in the construction and engineering industry, restaurants, the IT sector, teaching and finance with many taking up permanent residence in Singapore where they account for nearly a quarter of the Singapore population. The unskilled labour mainly work in Indian restaurants. There are also foreign spouses from the Indian Subcontinent who are married to local Indians.

## Education

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Education")]

There are significant differences between Malaysian Indians who are part of the Malaysian [working class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class "Working class") in plantations and urban middle class or upper middle class professionals. Many Indians are workers in plantations and attend Tamil language schools. Tamil language [primary schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Malaysia "Education in Malaysia") are funded by the [Federal Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Malaysia "Government of Malaysia"), but Tamil schools on private lands are not eligible for full government grant.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-24) Although Tamil is used as the medium of instruction, Malay and English are taught as compulsory subjects. Tamil schools may range from single classroom where students of varying age and ability are taught by a single teacher, to large schools of a few hundreds students. In plantation estates the schools may be poorly staffed and equipped, and few of their students moved on to higher education. Those Indians who do well in schools are primarily from the urban middle classes.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-25)

There are also significant challenges in higher education opportunity for Indians due to an education system that favors the Bumiputera Malays.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:0-9) Racial discrimination in government school have been reported, with Indian students suffering the most due to their ethnicity, color of the skin, or beliefs.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:0-9)

## Economy and employment

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Economy and employment")]

There are considerable differences socially and economically between different groups of Indians in Malaysia. They form a significant part of the professional classes in Malaysia; in a census taken in 1999, Malaysian Indians were 15.5% of Malaysia's professional workforce.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Haque-8) These include doctors (28.4%), lawyers (26.8%), dentists (21%), veterinary surgeons (28.5%), engineers (6.4%), accountants (5.8%), surveyors (3.0%) and architects (1.5%).[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-26) However, many Indians are also ranked amongst the most disadvantaged. In 1970, around 39% of Indians in Malaysia were ranked among the poorest of the country.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Haque-8) Incidence of poverty (those in the lowest 40% of the national income distribution) in Malaysia was 27.3% Indians.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-FOOTNOTESandhu2006375-27) Official figures suggest that the percentage of Indians living below the official poverty line has decreased from 39.2% in 1970 to 1.9% in 2002, but the figures have been criticised as inaccurate.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-28) It has been claimed around 40% of Indians are at the bottom rung of the income ladder in 2015.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-malaymail2015-6)

[![Image 14](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/KITLV_-_79935_-_Kleingrothe%2C_C.J._-_Medan_-_Sowing_on_a_rubber_plantation_in_Malaysia_-_circa_1910.tif/lossy-page1-250px-KITLV_-_79935_-_Kleingrothe%2C_C.J._-_Medan_-_Sowing_on_a_rubber_plantation_in_Malaysia_-_circa_1910.tif.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KITLV_-_79935_-_Kleingrothe,_C.J._-_Medan_-_Sowing_on_a_rubber_plantation_in_Malaysia_-_circa_1910.tif)

Workers in a plantation c. 1910

Many of the early South Indian immigrants such as the Tamils and Telugus worked in the agriculture sector, in particular as workers in plantations. In 1930, 60% of all Indians were in this sector, and by 1970, 45% of Indians were still involved in agriculture in Malaysia.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-FOOTNOTESandhu2006162-29)[Sri Lanka Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamil_people "Sri Lankan Tamil people") mainly occupied the subordinate administrative and technical positions in estates, positions they still dominate to this day.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-FOOTNOTESandhu2006166-30) Also in the estates are the Malayalis who hold assistant and managerial positions. Few of these estates however are Indian-owned.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-FOOTNOTESandhu2006167-31) Indians are also prominent in the transport and communication sector, either as labourers or as administrative and technical staff.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-FOOTNOTESandhu2006172%E2%80%93173-32)

Many Indians are also involved in business and trade, the majority of the privately owned businesses belong to the [Chettiars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chettiar "Chettiar").[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-FOOTNOTESandhu2006173%E2%80%93175-33) The Sikhs and other Punjabis were initially recruited by the British to serve in the police and armed forces, and while in modern times these areas are now dominated by Malays, the descendants of Sikhs and other Punjabi are now found in every sphere of the economy, in particular as professionals, and in the mercantile and money-lending business.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-FOOTNOTESandhu2006162-29)

Institutionalized racism in economic policy by the government is a key economic challenge for Indians. Many state governments have a no-bin policy which indirectly implies that if a non-Malay applies for anything it will be rejected.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:3-34)[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:0-9)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-35) According to the [Ninth Malaysia Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_Malaysia_Plan "Ninth Malaysia Plan") report of 2006, Malaysian Indians also controlled only 1.2 percent of the corporate wealth in Malaysia, down from the previous figure of 1.5 percent.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:4-12)[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-36) Although the Malaysian Indians are estimated at 9% of the population, they own just 2% of the national wealth due to racially discriminatory policies of institutionalized discrimination, such as the NEP.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-37)[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:2-38)[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-39)

## Politics

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Politics")]

[![Image 15](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Headquarters_of_MIC.JPG/250px-Headquarters_of_MIC.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Headquarters_of_MIC.JPG)

Headquarters of MIC in [Kuala Lumpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Lumpur "Kuala Lumpur").

The [Malaysian Indian Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indian_Congress "Malaysian Indian Congress") (MIC) is the oldest and largest Indian political party in Malaysia. It is a senior member of the [Barisan Nasional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisan_Nasional "Barisan Nasional") coalition. The [Indian Progressive Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Malaysian_Indian_Progressive_Front "All Malaysian Indian Progressive Front") (IPF), another Indian-based party, is affiliated with Barisan Nasional but is not a formal member. The [People's Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Progressive_Party_(Malaysia) "People's Progressive Party (Malaysia)") (PPP) is technically a multiracial party but its membership is overwhelmingly Indian. It is a member of the Barisan Nasional. The [People's Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Justice_Party_(Malaysia) "People's Justice Party (Malaysia)") (PKR) and [Democratic Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Action_Party "Democratic Action Party") (DAP) have a large Indian membership and have many Indian lawmakers. Both parties are members of the ruling [Pakatan Harapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakatan_Harapan "Pakatan Harapan") coalition. The [Socialist Party of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Malaysia "Socialist Party of Malaysia") (PSM), a minor opposition party, has a strong Indian presence. The [Hindu Rights Action Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Rights_Action_Force "Hindu Rights Action Force") (Hindraf) was formed in 2007 to address alleged racial discrimination against Indian Malaysians. It was banned after it staged a large anti-government rally in [Kuala Lumpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Lumpur "Kuala Lumpur") in 2007. Hindraf's political wing is the [Human Rights Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Party_(Malaysia) "Human Rights Party (Malaysia)") (HRP). The [Malaysian Indian Muslim Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indian_Muslim_Congress "Malaysian Indian Muslim Congress") (KIMMA) is a party that represents the interests of the [Indian Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Muslim "Indian Muslim") community. There is the [Punjabi Party of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Party_of_Malaysia "Punjabi Party of Malaysia") (PPM) which represents Malaysia's Punjabi community. The [Malaysian Ceylonese Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Ceylonese_Congress "Malaysian Ceylonese Congress") (MCC) represents Malaysia's [Sri Lankan Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Tamil "Sri Lankan Tamil") community who are technically not Indian but often regarded as such by most Malaysians. Other Indian fringe parties include the pro-Barisan Nasional [Malaysian Indian United Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indian_United_Party "Malaysian Indian United Party") (MIUP), [Malaysia Makkal Sakti Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Makkal_Sakti_Party "Malaysia Makkal Sakti Party") (MMSP) and the latest [Malaysian Indian Justice Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indian_Justice_Party "Malaysian Indian Justice Party") (MIJP). There was also the [Democratic Malaysian Indian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Malaysian_Indian_Party "Democratic Malaysian Indian Party") (DMIP) which has been dissolved.

In the [Malaysian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_general_election,_2018 "Malaysian general election, 2018") held in 2018, sixteen Indians were elected to the [Dewan Rakyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewan_Rakyat "Dewan Rakyat"). This is the [highest number of Malaysian Indians elected to parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Indians_in_the_Dewan_Rakyat "Ethnic Indians in the Dewan Rakyat") in Malaysian history.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-40)

[HINDRAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HINDRAF "HINDRAF") was an ally to the [Pakatan Harapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakatan_Harapan "Pakatan Harapan") government. This is in addition to [DAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Action_Party_(Malaysia) "Democratic Action Party (Malaysia)") and [PKR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Justice_Party_(Malaysia) "People's Justice Party (Malaysia)") which are multiracial parties with Indian representatives.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-41) However HINDRAF is dissolved as of 2019 by RoS and currently this ruling is being brought to court for hearing.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-42)

## Cuisine

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Cuisine")]

Further information: [Malaysian Indian Cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indian_Cuisine "Malaysian Indian Cuisine")

[![Image 17](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3b/Bananaleafrice001.jpg/250px-Bananaleafrice001.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bananaleafrice001.jpg)

[Banana leaf rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_leaf_rice "Banana leaf rice").

The contribution of the Indian community to [Malaysian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_cuisine "Malaysian cuisine") is substantial. Indian cuisine has had a strong influence on traditional [Malay cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_cuisine "Malay cuisine") resulting in the popularity of curries in Malaysia. Indian restaurants are well received by Malaysians from all ethnic and religious backgrounds. They have become an important fixture in everyday Malaysian life and are the venue of choice for watching live televised football matches. [Mamak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamak_stall "Mamak stall") restaurants and stalls refer to eateries owned and staffed by [Indian Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Muslims "Indian Muslims"). The word 'Mamak' is sometimes erroneously used to describe any Indian restaurant. [Roti canai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti_canai "Roti canai"), [nasi kandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasi_kandar "Nasi kandar"), [maggi goreng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggi_goreng "Maggi goreng") and [pasembur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasembur "Pasembur") (Mamak rojak) are Indian dishes unique to Malaysia. Nasi kandar is sold exclusively in [Indian Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Muslim "Indian Muslim") restaurants and the recipes are closely guarded secrets. Unlike Indian cuisine in the United Kingdom and other Western countries which tend to focus on [North Indian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Indian_cuisine "North Indian cuisine"), Indian cuisine in Malaysia is largely based on [South Indian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Indian_cuisine "South Indian cuisine") as the Malaysian Indian diaspora is overwhelmingly Tamil, although some northern dishes, such as [tandoori chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tandoori_chicken "Tandoori chicken") and [naan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naan "Naan") bread, are common. Southern breakfast delicacies, such as [idli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idli "Idli"), [vadai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadai "Vadai") and [dosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dosa_(food) "Dosa (food)") (spelled in Malaysia as 'thosai'), are common. The [appam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appam "Appam") is a favourite breakfast dish in Tamil homes. _Idiyappam_ is known as [putu mayam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putu_mayam "Putu mayam") in Malay and usually sold by mobile motorcycle vendors. [Murukku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murukku "Murukku") is made to mark [Deepavali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepavali "Deepavali"). Meals of rice with various vegetable and meat dishes along with other condiments are served on banana leaves in restaurants for lunch and dinner, and also in Indian households during special occasions. Mutton ([goat meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat_meat "Goat meat")) is highly favoured and served as either _varuval_ (dry curry) or _peratal_ (curry with a thick gravy). Fried bitter gourd, banana chips, [papadam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papadam "Papadam"), [rasam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasam_(dish) "Rasam (dish)"), yoghurt and pickles are the usual condiments. Desserts and sweets include [payasam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payasam "Payasam"), [halva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halva "Halva"), [mysore pak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_pak "Mysore pak"), palgoa and ghee balls. Some Indian foods have been widely adopted and localised. This includes but not limited to [Murukku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murukku "Murukku"), [Adhirasam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhirasam "Adhirasam") and [Puttu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puttu "Puttu").

## Religion

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Religion")]

The Indian community, which mostly consists of [Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamils "Tamils"), follows Hinduism as the main faith. Hinduism and Buddhism were brought to the Malay Peninsula from India around the 2nd century AD. The Indian-influenced kingdoms of Kadaram ([Old Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_history_of_Kedah "Early history of Kedah")), and Ilangosagam ([Langkasuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkasuka "Langkasuka")) practised Hinduism and Buddhism during the rule of the Malay-[Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya "Srivijaya") and Tamil-[Chola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola "Chola") kingdoms.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Arasaratnam1970-44) The early few Indians arrived and assimilated quickly into the local population through intermarriage.

Hinduism is the most practiced religion amongst the Tamils, including both the major Hindu and Tamil pantheon of deities. Tamils of both Indian and Sri Lankan backgrounds practice Hinduism. Vast majority of the [Malayalees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malayali "Malaysian Malayali") and Telugus in Malaysia also practice Hinduism.

[Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism") is practiced mainly by [Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_people "Punjabi people"). Many Sindhis also worship in Sikh Gurdwaras in Malaysia. Christianity is practiced by a minority of Tamil and Malayali people. In Malaysia, most of the Indian Christians are either [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic"), [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostal "Pentecostal"), [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican "Anglican"), [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist "Methodist"), or [Lutheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran "Lutheran"). The majority of the [Malayalee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malayali "Malaysian Malayali") Christians are either [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic "Catholic") or [Syrian Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Thomas_Christians "Saint Thomas Christians").[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-45) Trade contact with the Tamils predate the Islamic period (c. 570-632 AD), or the birth of Islam. Indonesians and Malays learned of Islam initially through Gujarati merchants and further developed on by the Arab traders. Most of the Indian Muslim community has been absorbed into the larger Malay community due to their common religious background, with a substantially high level of assimilation and intermarriage between the communities. In Malaysia, [Tamil Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Muslim "Tamil Muslim") are often referred to as 'Mamaks' while [Malayalee Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_Muslims "Malabar Muslims") are known as 'Kakas' or 'Malabaris'.

## Festivals

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Festivals")]

[![Image 23](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Thaipusam_idols.jpg/250px-Thaipusam_idols.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Thaipusam_idols.jpg)

Idols carried in procession during Thaipusam at Batu Caves.

One of the biggest Hindu festivals in Malaysia is [Thaipusam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaipusam "Thaipusam"). Thaipusam is dedicated to the God [Murugan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murugan "Murugan") which occurs on the day in the [Tamil month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar "Hindu calendar") of _Thai_ (January–February) when the asterism _Poosam_ is on the ascendant. It is celebrated in grand style in the temples of, [George Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Town,_Penang "George Town, Penang"), [Ipoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipoh "Ipoh") and [Kuala Lumpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Lumpur "Kuala Lumpur") for three days.

In Kuala Lumpur, Thaipusam has become an almost national seat for _Poosam_ celebrations. The venue of the Kuala Lumpur celebrations is a picturesque shrine right inside a cave that lies many feet above the ground, and can only be approached by a steep climb. This place, known as [Batu Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batu_Caves "Batu Caves"), is about eight miles from the city, and a [chariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_car "Temple car") procession carrying the image of the deity to and from the place adds to the color and gaiety of the festival. Crowds from all over the country throng to the cave, including people of all classes and groups. It is above all a day of penance, on which many vows are fulfilled. A 42.7m high statue of Lord Murugan was built at Batu Caves and was unveiled in January 2006, having taken three years to construct.

One of the most significant rites performed is the carrying of the [kavadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavadi "Kavadi"), a large wooden decorated arch, as an act of penance. When deities are taken on procession from one shrine to another, they would be followed by a number of these voluntary kavadi-bearers. In other towns and estates, kavadis would be carried for other festivals like _Chittirai Paruvam_. Some of the more rigid practitioners would bear spikes, spears, and hooks pierced into their bodies. The _Chittirai Paruvam_ festival and festivals to the Tamil deity [Mariamman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariamman "Mariamman") are usually accompanied by a fire-walking ceremony.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Arasaratnam1970-44)

[Deepavali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepavali "Deepavali"), popularly known as the 'Festival of Lights', is another major Hindu festival which is celebrated by all Hindu communities. Tamils celebrate [Pongal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pongal_(festival) "Pongal (festival)") which is a harvest festival usually held from 13–16 January. A similar festival known as [Makar Sankranti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makar_Sankranti "Makar Sankranti") is celebrated by most other Indian communities while the Punjabis call their harvest festival [Lohri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lohri "Lohri").

The [Malayalees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malayali "Malaysian Malayali") celebrate [Vishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishu "Vishu"), the Malayalee New Year which usually falls in the month of April or the month of Medam in the [Malayalam calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam_calendar "Malayalam calendar"). [Onam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onam "Onam") is the most popular festival celebrated by the Malayalee community and is usually observed in the month of August or September. They usually prepare _Sadhya_, a lunch feast consisting of 16 to 24 vegetarian dishes (without onions and garlics).[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-46)

The Telugus celebrate [Ugadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugadi "Ugadi"), the Telugu New Year which is based on the [lunar calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_calendar "Lunar calendar"); unlike [Puthandu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puthandu "Puthandu"), the Tamil New Year and [Vasakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasakhi "Vasakhi"), the Sikh New Year which are based on the [solar calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_calendar "Solar calendar"). [Makar Sankranti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makar_Sankranti "Makar Sankranti") is an important festival for [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_people "Telugu people") community also celebrated as Pongal by Tamils.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

The festivals celebrated by Malaysian Indians who profess the Christian faith are [Easter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter "Easter"), [Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas "Christmas") and [All Souls' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Souls'_Day "All Souls' Day") (mainly celebrated by Catholics). Indian Muslims observe [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan "Ramadan"), the Muslim month of fasting; and celebrate [Hari Raya Aidilfitri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Raya_Aidilfitri#South-East_Asia "Hari Raya Aidilfitri") and [Hari Raya Aidiladha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_al-Adha "Eid al-Adha").

## Influence on Malaysian culture

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Influence on Malaysian culture")]

Indian influences can be seen on modern Malaysia as well as on traditional Malay culture. The diffusion of Indian culture includes the following:

*   [Malay folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_folklore "Malay folklore") contains a rich number of Indian-influenced mythological characters, such as [Bidadari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidadari "Bidadari"), [Jentayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatayu "Jatayu"), [Garuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garuda "Garuda") and [Naga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C4%81ga "Nāga").
*   [Early Malay literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_literature "Malaysian literature") was highly influenced by Indian epics, such as the _[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata")_ and the _[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana")_.
*   [Wayang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayang "Wayang") shadow puppets and classical dance-dramas of Malaysia took stories from episodes of _Ramayana_ and _Mahabharata_.

### Language and names

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Language and names")]

Main article: [Tamil and Sanskrit inscriptions in the Malay world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_and_Sanskrit_inscriptions_in_the_Malay_world "Tamil and Sanskrit inscriptions in the Malay world")

A good number of Tamil inscriptions as well as Hindu and Buddhist icons emanating from South India have been found in Southeast Asia (and even in parts of [south China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_and_southern_China "Northern and southern China")). On the Malay Peninsula, inscriptions have been found at [Takuapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuapa "Takuapa"), not far from the [Vishnuite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnuite "Vishnuite") statues of Khao Phra Narai in [Southern Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Thailand "Southern Thailand"). It is a short inscription indicating that an artificial lake named _Avani-naranam_ was dug by _nangur-Udaiyan_ which is the name of an individual who possessed a military [fief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief "Fief") at Nangur, being famous for his abilities as a warrior, and that the lake was placed under the protection of the members of the Manikkiramam (which according to K.A. Nilakanta Sastri, was a merchant guild) living in the military camp.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Sastri1949-66)

An inscription dated 779 AD has been found in [Ligor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhon_Si_Thammarat_Kingdom "Nakhon Si Thammarat Kingdom"), Malay Peninsula. This refers to the trade relationship between the [Tamil country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Tamil_country "Ancient Tamil country") and Malaya.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Arokiaswamy2000-67) In [ancient Kedah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bujang_Valley "Bujang Valley") there is an inscription found by Dr. [Quaritch Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Geoffrey_Quaritch_Wales "Horace Geoffrey Quaritch Wales"). It is an inscribed stone bar, rectangular in shape, bears the ye-dharmma formula[[nb 1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-68) in [South Indian characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatteluttu_alphabet "Vatteluttu alphabet") of the 4th century AD, thus proclaiming the Buddhist character of the shrine near the find-spot (site I) of which only the basement survives. The inscriptions are on three faces in _Pallava Grantha script_. The Ruler Raja Ganga fled from his empire into the forests with his queen and an infant heir. Raja Ganga left traces of hideout on a nearby hill in form of artefacts on stones. All these inscriptions, both [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language "Tamil language") and [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") ones, relate to the activities of the people and rulers of the Tamil country of South India. The [Tamil inscriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_script "Tamil script") are at least four centuries posterior to the Sanskrit inscriptions, from which the early Tamils themselves were patronizers of the Sanskrit language.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Arokiaswamy2000-67) The Cherok Tokun Ancient Inscriptions were first documented by Colonel [James Low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Low_(East_India_Company_officer) "James Low (East India Company officer)"), a British army officer, in 1845. In his log, Low recorded his disappointment of not finding a more spectacular ruin, expecting to find an ancient temple ruin. He documented what he made out to be "a group of seven inscriptions". The inscriptions were believed to be in pre-Pallava script and written in Sanskrit. They were attributed to the ancient Kingdom of Kadaaram, which flourished in northern Malaysia in the 5th to 6th centuries. However, according to J Laidlay, who translated the text in 1848, the inscription was in fact written in [Pali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali "Pali") - another ancient language of the Indian subcontinent.

An element needed to carry out commercial transactions is a common language understood by all parties involved in early trade. Historians such as J.V. Sebastian, K.T. Thirunavukkarasu, and A.W. Hamilton record that Tamil was the common language of commerce in Malaysia and Indonesia during historical times.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Arokiaswamy2000-67) The maritime Tamil significance in Sumatran and Malay Peninsula trading continued for centuries and borrowings into Malay from Tamil increased between the 15th and 19th centuries due to their commercial activities. In the 17th century, the Dutch East India Company was obliged to use Tamil as part of its correspondence. In Malacca and other seaports up to the 19th century, Malay terminology pertaining to book-keeping and accountancy was still largely Tamil.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-Sneddon2003-14)

### Infrastructure

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Infrastructure")]

Indians have contributed significantly to the building of Malaysia since the 19th century. The Indian workforce was instrumental in the clearing of land for infrastructure, established rubber plantations, built the roads, set up transmission lines as well as managed early Malayan railways, ports and airports. Indian doctors, chemists and veterinarians formed the bulk of medical personnel in Malaysia - their contributions still persist to present day. Indian civil servants formed the core of the civil service both pre- and post-independence. Indian teachers who were particularly fluent in the English language formed the backbone of Malaysian education, particularly in missionary schools. Indians also pioneered private education in Malaysia.

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Diaspora")]

A number of Malaysian Indian immigrants are found in Australia, and they tended to be highly qualified and speak English fluently and were therefore highly employable.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-69) There is also a small community of [Australians of Indian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Australian "Indian Australian") on the [Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia")[external territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_territories_of_Australia "States and territories of Australia") of [Christmas Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island "Christmas Island") who live alongside other Australians of ethnic Malaysian Chinese, Malay and European descent.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-70)

## Challenges for the future

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malaysian_Indians&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Challenges for the future")]

Institutionalized racism and discrimination in government policy such as [NEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_New_Economic_Policy "Malaysian New Economic Policy") had an adverse impact on non-Malays including the Malaysian Indian minority in Malaysia.[[71]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:0a-72) Despite the fact that the average income of Malaysian Indians exceeds that of their Malay counterparts, there exists a substantial portion of the community who are poor, with 40% of Malaysian Indians claimed to be at the bottom rung of the income ladder.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-malaymail2015-6) Despite the obvious need of the poor, Malaysian Indians are not eligible for any of Malaysia's affirmative-action programmes, which are reserved for Malays and select indigenous people.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:2-38)[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-ragesh-73)[Ramon Navaratnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Navaratnam "Ramon Navaratnam") argued the NEP needs to be reformed as it is outdated, and should be replaced with a system that can help close the gap of poverty regardless of race and religion.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:1-11)[[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-74)

Further challenges faced by the Indian community is religious conflict due to increasing Islamisation,[[72]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-ragesh-73) and there were communal clashes between Indians and Malays such as the [2001 Kampung Medan riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Kampung_Medan_riots "2001 Kampung Medan riots"),[[74]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-75) as well as protest over racial discrimination and temple demolitions during the [2007 HINDRAF rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_HINDRAF_rally "2007 HINDRAF rally").[[75]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-76)[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-:3-34) These factors in part have resulted in the migration of many highly skilled Malaysian Indians abroad, where Indian migrants are largely upwardly mobile. A survey in 2017 found that 42% of Malaysian Indians who had completed their secondary or tertiary education wanted to emigrate.[[76]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians#cite_note-77)
