# What is retrieval practice?

Retrieval practice is a strategy in which**bringing information to mind enhances and boosts learning**. Deliberately recalling information forces us to pull our knowledge “out” and examine what we know.

[![Image 4: Pooja K. Agarwal, Ph.D.](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/564a53ace4b0ef1eb2daff41/1449613381180-OAECF76XSUY20QEQ0TPL/Logo+NO+Text+Color.png?format=1500w)](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/)

# When we think about learning, we typically focus on getting information **into**students’ heads. What if, instead, we focus on getting information **out** of students’ heads?

* * *

Retrieval practice boosts learning by pulling information **_out_** of students’ heads, rather than cramming information into students’ heads. Just like students practice an instrument, they need to practice their knowledge, too.

We tend to think that most learning occurs during the initial encoding stage–when students get information “in” by re-reading, reviewing, and taking notes. However, one of the most robust findings from 100+ years of cognitive science research is that a significant amount of learning occurs when students pull information “out” by using a strategy called retrieval practice.

> Retrieval practice is a learning strategy, **not** an assessment strategy.

[Here are quick research-based teaching tips](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/strategies) that **don’t** require additional lesson prep or grading:

*   Rather than starting class by reviewing content (“here’s what we did in class last week”), [simply ask students to retrieve](https://youtu.be/OUl0mMVbkAc?t=331) (“what did we do in class last week?”). This tiny switch from encoding to retrieval practice will boost your students’ long-term learning and reduce their forgetting.

*   [Brain dumps](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/braindump): ask students to write down everything they can remember from a previous lesson

*   [Two things](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/twothings): ask students to retrieve two things they learned at the end of class

## Unleash the power of retrieval practice

> Research demonstrates that retrieval practice can increase students’ grades from a C to an A.

[Research by cognitive scientists](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/scientists) demonstrates that retrieval practice consistently boosts student learning. [A recent review of the literature](https://retrievalpractice.org/review) found benefits for a diverse range of:

*   Student populations (K−12 to medical school)

*   Subject areas (e.g., history to CPR skills)

*   Long-term assessments (days, weeks, and months)

*   Developmental stages (preschool, young adults, and older adults)

[Why retrieval practice improves learning and reduces forgetting](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/faq):

*   It helps students “use it or lose it,” just like practicing a language or an instrument

*   With retrieval practice, struggling is a **good** thing for learning (what scientists call a “desirable difficulty”)

*   Retrieval practice improves students’ understanding of their own learning process (what scientists call “metacognition”)

Learn more about retrieval practice from cognitive scientist and founder of retrievalpractice.org, Pooja K. Agarwal, Ph.D.
